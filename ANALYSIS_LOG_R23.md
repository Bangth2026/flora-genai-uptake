## FLoRA (src/r23_flora_xsub.py, src/r23_flora.py)

- F-51. Partial identification. Each token is one of C, echo that entered the editor only after a reply containing it (E-after), other echo (E-before), X-with-C (the reporting partition of Table 5 of the article, recomputed by `src/r23_flora_xsub.py` with the window embeddings replaced by zeros, which cannot change X-with-C because it precedes both embedding flags; agreement with the released labels 99.94%), other X, T and S. Assumptions: A1-A3; N (no coincidence); B (chatbot text edited before entry enters only in the same insertion as matched chatbot text); R (typed text is not chatbot text retyped with changes). Share of writers at or above half, with 95% student-bootstrap intervals (2,000 resamples, seed 2301): lower end C 20.0% [14.9, 24.7]; C + E-after 23.6% [18.5, 28.7]; upper end under all assumptions, C + E-after + X-with-C, 37.5% [32.0, 43.3]; without B, C + E-after + all X, 81.8% [77.1, 86.2]; without B and A1, C + all E + all X, 92.0% [88.4, 94.9]; without any assumption 100%. Mean shares of the proposal: C 23.6%, E-after 3.9%, E-before 9.7%, X-with-C 12.3%, X 50.9%, T 11.7%, S 0.2%. The counts of Table 4 of the eighth version are reproduced: C + large-entry echo 33.5%, C + X-with-C 34.5%, both 47.3%. Per-writer category shares: `results/r23_flora_students.csv` (derived from FLoRA, CC BY 4.0).
- F-52. Data-driven entry threshold. 84,481 insertions of the 63 keystroke students with insertions; typed = printable editor keystrokes (window of the R9 audit) for at least half of the inserted characters (69,620 insertions). The 99th percentile of the sizes of typed insertions is 2 weighted by tokens (student bootstrap 1 to 4, 500 resamples) and 1 weighted by insertions. Mean X share: 53.5% (tau = 1), 52.3% (2), 50.9% (5), 48.9% (13), 47.3% (20).
- F-53. Census of every C run of at least eight tokens of the keystroke students: 354 runs, 57 students, 13,418 tokens. Selected in the chatbot panel before entry 81.6% [74.0, 88.2] of runs (95.0% of their tokens); not typed 84.7% [76.7, 91.5]; typed 5.6%; preceded by a paste shortcut 22.3%; carried by a logged paste 88.6% of the 352 runs of students with logged pastes. Of the 65 runs not selected, 10.8% were typed and 78.5% had neither keystrokes nor a paste shortcut.
- F-54. The seven C passages of the R9 audit sample not selected in the panel: one retyped (keystrokes for 42 of 47 characters, in no logged paste); five carried by a logged paste (three with a paste shortcut); one with no evidence either way. None near an opening of the separate chatbot window.

## NIRVANA (src/r23_nirvana_agg.py)

When this entry was written the OSF project 3a8uh was no longer publicly accessible, and a request for access was not granted. The analyses below are computed from the stored outputs of R22 (`results/r22_nirvana.json`, `results/r22_nirvana_posthoc.json`); nothing was re-run on the raw data.

- N-10. Tokens entered by a paste matched to a copy from a ChatGPT reply: 12,185 (41.0%), of which 96.2% labelled C. All other tokens: 17,507, of which 2,770 (15.8%) labelled C (typed 1,384; pasted without a copy event 1,004; other 382); the copy signal is silent on them. 80.9% of C lies in the first stratum.
- N-11. Naive 8-gram overlap marks at least 99.0% of the copied tokens; 73.9% of the tokens it marks were copied (C: 80.9%). Spearman correlation with the dataset's share of pasted ChatGPT text: U 0.88 [0.77, 0.95], naive 0.85 [0.74, 0.93], paired difference 0.03 [-0.03, 0.09] (2,000 resamples, seed 2302). Echo: 344 of 1,838 tokens (18.7%) copied, 1,494 not.
- N-12. Coincidence with the writer's own replies is at most 19.1% of C (C without a copy event). The 5.3% of the eighth version (typed C in runs shorter than 12 tokens) is a bound only if typed runs of 12 or more tokens are retyped reply passages and C pasted without a copy event came from a reply; neither premise can be tested from the stored outputs.
- Not run: sensitivity of N-1 to N-4 to the copy-matching threshold (0.3, 0.5, 0.8) and to excluding the essay-prompt pane from H. Both need the raw data. The student-level comparison with the dataset's own measure depends on neither.

## Not run: a hosted model of the same family

A first estimate of the rate of A3 violations in FLoRA would send the writers' prompts to GPT-4o (or a successor) at the temperature used in FLoRA and measure verbatim coverage of C and X. No access to a hosted model was available for this entry, so the A3 violation rate in FLoRA remains unknown and field C precision is not estimated.
