# Analysis log entry R11

Written after all earlier results (S1 to R10) were known, and committed before the R11 analyses ran. All analyses are exploratory. One decision changes the primary attribution: the entry mode that separates typed (T) from unresolved (X) text is decided per token (log entry R9, F-8, `src/r9b_flora_token_entry.py`) instead of per n-gram. C, E and S, and therefore U\*, are unchanged by this decision. The n-gram entry rule is kept and reported as a variant. All other parameter values are unchanged (n = 8; tau = 5 for FLoRA and 20 for RelianceScope; delta = 0; peer threshold 5; token priority C > E > S > T > X; alphanumeric tokens).

## P. Properties of the rules (src/r11_properties.py)

P-1. Soundness of C: an n-gram present in the editor before every reply that contains it is never labelled C. Checked on every n-gram of the 275 FLoRA proposals and of the simulated students (count of violations).
P-2. Monotonicity of the online version: a label assigned at entry changes at most once, from S, T or X to E, and a C label is never withdrawn. Checked on the simulated students and the 275 FLoRA students (transition counts between entry label and final label).
P-3. Determinism and traceability: two runs on the same logs give identical labels, and each label is stored with the log events that decided it (time of first reply, first turn, entry snapshot and its insertion size). Checked by running the attribution twice and comparing hashes of the outputs.

## B. Benchmark with planted provenance, extended (src/r11_benchmark.py)

B-4. Routes outside the specification. In addition to the 13 routes of R10 (unchanged), simulated students may use six routes that the rules were not designed for:
- C-edit-p: a reply passage pasted and then edited in place in the editor, p = 20% or 50% of its tokens replaced one at a time by typed tokens over later snapshots. True class: surviving reply tokens C, replaced tokens T.
- C-interleave: a reply passage entered in chunks of 8 to 30 tokens, alternately pasted from the reply and typed from the student's own text. True class: pasted chunks C, typed chunks T.
- C-regen: the chatbot answers the same turn twice (regeneration); both replies are logged, the second differs from the first in 15% of its tokens, and the student pastes from the second. True class C.
- C-regen-unlogged: as C-regen, but only the first reply is logged. True class C.
- C-stream: the student copies a reply passage while the reply is still streaming; the log records the reply at its completion, 2 to 20 s after the paste. True class C. Scored with delta = 0 and delta = 30 s.
- U-undo: a typed block or a pasted chatbot block is deleted (undo) and restored 5 to 60 s later as one insertion (redo). True class: T for the typed block, C for the chatbot block.
Text copied from the same chatbot in another browser tab, whose dialogue is not logged, is the route X-ai of R10 and is not added.
B-5. Route mix. 50 random mixes over the 19 routes, weights drawn from a Dirichlet distribution centred on the R10 weights (concentration 13), 200 simulated students per mix; distribution (median, 5th and 95th percentiles) of token accuracy, precision, recall and F1 for C, and of the student-level error of U\*. Base mix with only the R10 routes and with all 19 routes.
B-6. Seeds and intervals. The R10 benchmark (13 routes, base mix) repeated over 20 seeds of 400 students; mean and 2.5th to 97.5th percentiles of every per-class score. For seed 2100: 95% intervals from 2,000 bootstrap resamples of simulated students for precision, recall and F1 of every class and every baseline, and for the difference in F1 between the attribution and each baseline (paired resamples).
B-7. Additional baselines on the same logs: (g) earliest plausible attribution in the manner of de Alfaro and Shavlovsky (2013) and WikiWho (Flöck and Acosta, 2014): every student turn, reply and editor snapshot is a revision with an author (student, chatbot, student); each 8-gram of the final text is attributed to the author of the earliest revision that contains it (ties to the chatbot) and projected to tokens with the same priority as the attribution; (h) a paste-log rule in the manner of Inputlog and Grammarly Authorship: tokens that enter the editor in an insertion of more than tau tokens are chatbot text if at least half of the 8-grams of that insertion occur in an earlier reply, typed if they enter in insertions of at most tau tokens, and unknown otherwise; (i) a small open instruction-tuned LLM (Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, greedy decoding, run locally on CPU) that receives, for each sentence of the final text, the sentence, the reply passage with the largest 8-gram overlap and the student turn with the largest overlap, both with times, and the time the sentence first appeared in the editor, and answers chatbot, student or neither; on 40 simulated students (seed 2100). A script for hosted models with the same prompt is released (`src/r11_llm_baseline.py`).
B-8. Entry rule. Per-class scores at full resolution and with snapshots thinned to 5, 15, 60 s, under the n-gram and the token entry rule, with tau = 5 and tau = 20.
B-9. Selective prediction. For the binary decision "chatbot text", the attribution abstains on X; baselines abstain where their evidence is weakest. Confidence of a token: for the attribution, X lowest, then the number of covering 8-grams that carry the token's label; for overlap baselines, the distance of the share of covering 8-grams found in replies from 0.5. Risk-coverage curves (error among covered tokens against the share covered) and the area under each curve, with true X tokens scored as not chatbot text.

## F. FLoRA (src/r11_flora.py)

F-1. Primary class shares, keystroke audit and per-student class file under the token entry rule; the n-gram entry rule as a variant.
F-2. Unresolved text. For the 68 students with logged pastes: logged pastes of at least 8 tokens grouped by the class that holds most of their tokens (C, X, mixed C and X, other); length, time before the last snapshot, time since the last reply, and share entering within 60 s of a reply, by group. For all 275 students: the share of C, X and T tokens entering in the last 24 h before the last snapshot, and the X share by quartile of task time.
F-3. Shared-source n-grams that also occur in a reply that came before their first editor appearance (the n-grams that rule 2 takes away from C).
F-4. Clock direction: signed difference between the logged ask time and the nearest preceding Enter keystroke in the chat box, and the share of chatbot-first n-grams that would change class under delta = -5, -2, +2, +5 s.
F-5. Clustering of the keystroke audit: number of students per class and intraclass correlation of agreement within students (one-way random effects), for the R9 audit sample and the token entry rule.
